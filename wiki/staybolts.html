Staybolts

Staybolts tie a locomotive boiler's firebox sheets to the outer wrapper sheet, so both can resist boiler pressure across the water space.

Flexible staybolt assemblages (Flannery, 1938)

The Flannery Bolt Company 1938 catalogue groups its products as follows (see Flannery 1938 stay catalogue, pp. 4–5):

  • Assemblages: two-piece (welded cap, MK and MK-S); three-piece (welded sleeve); round nut (welded sleeve); three-piece and round nut (threaded sleeve).
  • Bolts: flexible (round head); crown (taper and buttonhead); rigid (straight body and reduced body).
  • Sleeves, round nuts and caps: welded and threaded types.
  • Installation and testing tools: including testing tools for tell-tale flexible bolts.

The MK cap covers the bolt head in what Flannery calls its simplest flexible assemblage. The taller MK-S cap is for boilers with thinner wrapper sheets, so the recommended clearance between bolt head and cap is kept. The two-piece assemblage uses Type K, KK, KJ, KH or KG bolts, which must be the tell-tale type (see Flannery 1938 stay catalogue, p. 8). The exact sheet-thickness limits are printed on that page, but the OCR can't read them reliably; check the original scan.

On specific locomotives

  • SAR Class K: separate drawings for the boiler stays (A9), the longitudinal stay carrier (A10), the firebox stay diagram (A13) and the firebox stays (A14). The firebox sheet shows a copper stay at full size, with 11 threads per inch (see Set - Original SAR - PDF, files 0012, 0013, 0016–0018).
